Introduction to loan writeback

Simplify your implementation with our loan writeback best practices guide and correctly account for a loan programmatically

Overview​

This guide takes you through the steps needed to implement and run the loan writeback procedure in your lending business using Codat. You will learn how to configure Codat and use it to create transactions that represent the deposit and repayment of the loan in your customers' accounting software.

This solution covers the loan writeback procedure for both general lending, such as term loans, and selective invoice finance.

What is loan writeback?​

Loan writeback (also known as lending writeback) is the process of continuously updating an accounting software with information about a loan. It helps maintain an accurate position of the loan during the entire lending cycle by recording the loan liability, any interest, fees, or repayments, and facilitating the reconciliation of bank accounts.

Bank feeds authorization

For some accounting software, you must obtain approval to integrate bank feedsBank Feeds A Codat product that enables automatic synchronization of bank transaction data into a company's accounting software. prior to implementing loan writeback.

Why use it?​

A bookkeeper can account for a loan in numerous ways in an accounting software. For example, some bookkeepers may erroneously register a loan as a direct income or even a sales invoice.

This results in loans being improperly recorded as revenue and repayments as operating costs. At the end of the reporting period, this can make it hard for the bookkeeper to close their books.

By implementing loan writeback functionality in your application, you can make sure loan bookkeeping is done regularly, correctly, and quickly so that you always see an up-to-date state of the borrower's accounts.

Critically, the loan writeback functionality helps lenders clearly identify loans in an SMBSMB The primary customer segment that Codat helps businesses serve, typically companies with annual revenues under $500 million.'s accounting software. As a result, the lender sees a more accurate picture of the SMBSMB The primary customer segment that Codat helps businesses serve, typically companies with annual revenues under $500 million.'s debt repayment history, which leads to improved underwriting.

Mandatory loan writeback

Certain accounting software require lenders to continuously update their books with money lent to SMBsSMB The primary customer segment that Codat helps businesses serve, typically companies with annual revenues under $500 million.. For example, Xero obligates lenders going through the App Partner certification process to handle the writeback process.

What's the process?​

The process of loan writeback involves recording loan withdrawals, repayments, and interest in the SMBSMB The primary customer segment that Codat helps businesses serve, typically companies with annual revenues under $500 million.'s accounting software. It can be split into three stages, as shown on the diagram below:

  1. Configure loan writeback for your SMBSMB The primary customer segment that Codat helps businesses serve, typically companies with annual revenues under $500 million. customer.

  2. Deposit the loan and reflect it in the SMBSMB The primary customer segment that Codat helps businesses serve, typically companies with annual revenues under $500 million.'s accounting software.

  3. Record payments owed to you, the lender, in your SMBSMB The primary customer segment that Codat helps businesses serve, typically companies with annual revenues under $500 million.'s accounting software.

The process is identical for the general lending and invoice finance scenarios.

The lender agrees to provide an SMBSMB The primary customer segment that Codat helps businesses serve, typically companies with annual revenues under $500 million. a loan of £10,000 and charges a fee of £200, requesting the repayment in equal parts over 2 months.

The lender deposits £10,000 into the SMBSMB The primary customer segment that Codat helps businesses serve, typically companies with annual revenues under $500 million.'s nominated bank account. Next month, the borrower repays the first half of the loan and repays the loan in full the month after.

In the example, you can also see that each bank feed transaction matches a bank account transaction in the accounting software. This helps the borrower to reconcile both bank accounts.

The lender agrees to provide the SMBSMB The primary customer segment that Codat helps businesses serve, typically companies with annual revenues under $500 million. with an advance of £800 for a £1000 invoice with a £50 fee. The lender deposits £800 into the SMBSMB The primary customer segment that Codat helps businesses serve, typically companies with annual revenues under $500 million.'s bank account and the SMBSMB The primary customer segment that Codat helps businesses serve, typically companies with annual revenues under $500 million.'s customer pays for the invoice within the payment period.

When the SMBSMB The primary customer segment that Codat helps businesses serve, typically companies with annual revenues under $500 million. receives payment from the customer, the funds are transferred from the borrower's bank account into the lender's bank account, settling the loan and associated fees. The lender’s fee is then deducted from this payment.

As a result, the lender's account transactions balance to zero, and the advance and the outstanding amount remain in the borrower's account.
